# RefactorIA Team Charter

Roles clarify responsibilities and authority boundaries, not personal hierarchy. Technical discussion relies on arguments and evidence, and no role removes the context behind a decision.

The operational roster is intentionally excluded from this OSS repository. This charter does not record membership, access, invitations, or private-space structure.

## Roles and boundaries

| Role | Responsibility | Does not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | Sets direction, scope, publication, and final decisions. | Replacing technical discussion or disregarding evidence. |
| RefactorIA Coordinator | Organizes priorities, dependencies, and follow-through. | Technical veto, write access, merge authority, or moderation by default. |
| RefactorIA Team | Implements, reviews, and proposes improvements. | Final product decisions, publication, or administration. |
| Moderator | Supports healthy community conduct and applies community rules. | Product, technical, or repository authority by default. |
| Contributor | Brings ideas, reports, reviews, and public changes. | Operational access or moderation responsibilities. |

One person may hold more than one responsibility only when the scope and boundaries are clear. Concrete assignments belong in restricted operational records, not this public charter.

## Where work happens

| Space | Public responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| GitHub | Issues, pull requests, reviews, technical decisions, public history, and backlog. |
| Discord | Conversation, community coordination, and support. It does not replace GitHub's decision record. |

Decisions that change software, public documentation, or priorities must be traceable in GitHub. Discord can help a conversation move forward, but it does not replace evidence or review.

## Authority principles

- The Owner keeps final decision authority and considers the recorded context.
- The RefactorIA Coordinator organizes work without redefining other role boundaries.
- The RefactorIA Team may question, review, and propose using the same evidence standard.
- A Moderator acts on community conduct and safety, not product decisions by virtue of moderation.
- A Contributor participates through public mechanisms and can raise questions in the relevant issue or pull request.

## Working agreements

- Work is asynchronous; immediate responses are not assumed.
- Respectful disagreement improves outcomes when it adds verifiable context.
- Responsibility and technical access are separate; a role does not imply permissions.
- Sensitive changes follow the applicable public process and security documentation.

## Reader checklist

- [ ] I know which decisions belong in GitHub.
- [ ] I know when Discord is only coordination.
- [ ] I understand that roles do not grant implicit permissions.
- [ ] I do not expect rosters or operational details in this OSS repository.
